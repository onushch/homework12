Earth: The Third Planet from the Sun

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known astronomical object to support life. It formed approximately 4.54 billion years ago and is the fifth-largest planet in the Solar System by diameter and mass. Earth is a terrestrial planet, meaning it has a solid, rocky surface. About 71% of its surface is covered with water, mostly in the form of oceans, with the remaining 29% consisting of continents and islands. This unique combination of land, water, and a stable atmosphere creates conditions suitable for life. The planet has one natural satellite — the Moon — which stabilizes Earth's axial tilt and contributes to ocean tides. Earth’s atmosphere is primarily composed of nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%), with traces of argon, carbon dioxide, and other gases. This atmosphere protects life by filtering harmful solar radiation and regulating temperature through the greenhouse effect. Earth rotates on its axis once every 24 hours, creating the cycle of day and night, and orbits the Sun once every 365.25 days, defining one year. Its axial tilt of 23.5° causes seasonal variations across the planet. From space, Earth appears as a blue and white sphere due to its oceans and cloud-covered atmosphere — a symbol of life in the vast silence of the cosmos.
